What the experiments show

  • The weak motor forces pumping. With 42% of the torque needed to hold the pendulum horizontal, every successful controller runs the motor at its limit for most of the swing-up and needs about 2.1 s from hanging rest. Linearised LQR alone reaches 15% of starts on the pendulum and 10% on the cart-pole.
  • Pendulum: learned controllers land within 1–4% of the true optimum. DP gives the exact optimal cost from rest, 3.34. DDPG reaches 3.38, SAC 3.42, PPO 3.44, PIRL 3.46, PINN actor-critic 3.48 and X-TFC-PI 3.50. The hand-tuned energy+LQR controller is 9% above optimal (3.63), and sampling MPC with a 1.6 s horizon is 56% above (5.22) because it can't see a whole swing-up ahead.
  • Physics-informed solvers train in seconds to minutes. X-TFC policy iteration solves each step as one least-squares problem: 19 s for X-TFC-PI and 2.3 min for X-TFC-PIRL, versus 30–55 min for DDPG, TD3, SAC and PPO on the same machine.
  • The LQR-anchored critic fixes balancing. The anchor forces the value function to match the LQR solution at the top. Without it, PIRL catches the pendulum but parks it about 0.2 rad off vertical (0% success); with it, 100%. On X-TFC-PIRL it cuts the cost from rest from 3.97 to 3.53.
  • The physics term matters. The same integral-RL learner without the HJB residual (IRL) is 36% costlier from rest (4.69 vs 3.46). Directly minimising the HJB residual without policy iteration (PINN-HJB) converges to a spurious solution on both plants. Starting from a stabilising controller is essential.
  • Cart-pole is the harder test. SAC, PPO and DDPG reach 100% but need 0.6–5 million environment steps; TD3 and A2C never balance it. Unanchored PIRL reaches 95% after 2.5 minutes of training, and from hanging rest it beats energy+LQR (3.78 vs 4.23) and MPC (4.94). The anchor and the random-feature X-TFC critic, both helpful on the 2-D pendulum, do not carry over to the 4-D cart-pole with its rail limits.
